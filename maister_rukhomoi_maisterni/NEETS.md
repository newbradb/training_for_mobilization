# Conductors, semiconductors, insulators

Normally, **conductors** have three or less valence electrons; **insulators** have five or more valence electrons; and **semiconductors** usually have four valence electrons.

# Magnets 

- Q: How does the law of magnetic poles relate to the law of electric charges?
- A: Like poles repel, unlike poles attract. 

- Q:  Using Weber’s molecular theory of magnetism, describe the polarity of the magnetic poles
produced by stroking a magnetic material from right to left with the south pole of a magnet.
- A: The left end (where stroke finishes) becomes north pole, the right end (where stroke starts) become south. 

- Q: What is the difference between the domain theory and Weber’s theory of magnetism?
- Weber's theory says individual molecules turn to line up. Domain theory says whole regions of atoms (domains), magnetized by electron spin, grow and rotate to line up. Domain theory also explains why only certein materials can be magnetized.

- Q: What is a magnetic line of force?
- A: Imaginary line in magnetic fied that shows direction of force. 

- Q: In what way do magnetic lines of force differ from electrostatic lines of force?
- A: Electrostatic line of force has start and end. Magnetic line are in closed loops.  

- Q31. How should a delicate instrument be protected from a magnetic field? 
- A: Surround instrument with a soft iron shield (magnetic shield); the magnetic lines of force pass through the iron instead of through instrument. 

- Q32. How should bar magnets be stored?
Store magnet bars in the pairs, oposite poles together, with soft iron keepers in  the ends. This provides closed, low relucatnce for the flux. So magnets keep their strength. 

# Electrical Energy

**Energy** is ability to do work. In order to do the work energy must be expended (converted from one form to another). 

- Energy contained by an object due its motion is called **kinetik energy**
- Energy contained by an object due its position is called **potential energy**

**Coulomb** unit adopted for measuring charges. When a charge of one coulomb exists between two bodies, one unit of electrical potential energy exists, which is called the difference of potential between two bodies. This is **Electromotive force**, or **Voltage**, unit of measure is **Volt**. A difference of potential can exists only between points with different charges. 

Foundamental law of electricity is **Electron flow is directly proportioional to the applied voltage**. If the voltage is increased, the flow is increased. If the voltage decreased, the flow is decreased.

There are six known methods for producing voltage : 

1. Friction - Voltage produced by rubbing certain materials together. 
2. Pressure (piezoelectricity) - Voltage produced by squeezing crystals of certain substances. 
3. Heat (thermoelectricity) - Voltage produced by heating the joint where two unlike metals are joined. 
4. Light (photoelectricity) - Voltage produced by light striking photosensitive substances. 
5. Chemical Action - Voltage produced by chemical reaction in battery cell. 
6. Magnetism - Voltage produced in conductor when the conductor moves through magnetic field.
